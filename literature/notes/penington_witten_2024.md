# Penington & Witten (2024) — Algebras and states in super-JT gravity

**Citation.** G. Penington, E. Witten, *Algebras and states in super-JT gravity*, arXiv:2412.15549 [hep-th] (v1, Dec 2024;
"Prepared for submission to JHEP"; no journal reference known to us, and G. Lin 2026 ref. [49] cites the arXiv version
only). File: `papers/penington_witten_2024.pdf`.

What was read (2026-10-27):
- §1 in full;
- §4.2–4.4: the right-boundary algebra, ground states, simultaneous ground states;
- §5.3–5.5: the projected algebra, charged operators and BPS counts, summary;
- App. D in full and the start of App. E.

What was skimmed only: §2–3 (bosonic JT, a review plus a Lorentzian proof) and App. A–C.

**Role in this project.** This is the rigorous canonical-quantization account of the zero-energy sector of N=2 super-JT
with matter, which is the gravitational home of LMRS operators P O P.

Three outputs matter for us:
1. The exact count: one zero-energy state per matter multiplet, inside a charge window.
2. The algebra of projected operators is closed and explicit.
3. The trace on that algebra weights the R-charge sectors by cos(πJ_R), which encodes the BPS counting.

## Main question
In N=2 JT gravity coupled to matter:
- What are the states with zero energy at both boundaries?
- What is the algebra of boundary observables once it is projected onto them?

## Physical system
- N=2 super-JT gravity with gauge supergroup SU(1,1|1) (universal covers implied). The bulk matter QFT carries SU(1,1|1)
  representations, Hilbert space H_matt.
- Two boundaries, each with supercharges Q, Q†, Hamiltonian H = {Q, Q†} and R-charge J.

## Important definitions
- Matter SU(1,1|1) representations (§4.4) are labelled by λ, the lowest eigenvalue of j₁, and by u, the R-charge of that
  state.
  - Long ("ordinary"): |u| < λ, made of four discrete series of SL(2,R), with lowest weights λ, λ+½, λ+½, λ+1 (4.108–4.112).
  - BPS: λ = |u|, made of two discrete series (4.113–4.120).
- Ṽ_{J_R}: H_matt → H_super-JT, the isometry onto the zero-energy states of fixed right R-charge J_R (4.97). It exists for
  |J_R| < ½.
- Pure super-JT zero-energy states |Ψ̃_{J_R}⟩ exist for |J_R| < ½, with norm factor √cos(πJ_R) (4.96).
- Projected operators Φ̃ⁱ_R = Σ Ṽ† Φⁱ_R Ṽ (5.45). The projected algebra Ã_{R,0} is generated by the neutral primaries.

## Main assumptions
- Canonical quantization with the gauge constraints imposed exactly.
- The matter theory is generic enough that the projected algebra is a **factor**. This is needed for faithfulness and
  uniqueness of the trace, and assumed, not proven (§5.3, §5.5).
- Supersymmetric primaries with matter R-charge |q| < ½ give nonzero projected operators.

## Main analytical results
1. **Simultaneous ground states** (§4.4; intro eq. 1.14). Each SU(1,1|1) matter representation contains exactly one
   normalizable state with E_L = E_R = 0, provided |J̃_L| < ½ with J̃_L = 2u − J_R.
   - This holds for BPS representations (4.127, 4.140) and also for long ones (4.152–4.153 and the argument after 4.155).
   - For long representations the state breaks all supersymmetry in the bulk but preserves it at both boundaries
     (abstract).
2. **Continuum thresholds** (4.133–4.135, 4.156). Above the ground states, H̃_L has a continuum starting at
   (J̃_L ∓ ½)²/4 for states annihilated by Q̃_L or Q̃_L† (4.134–4.135, BPS u > 0).
   - The text says these "exactly match" pure super-JT. Note: the long-representation version (4.156) is written with
     1/8, and (4.143) has (J̃_L − 1/4)², which looks like a typo for ½. The normalization therefore differs between
     equations; we quote the zero of the threshold, not its coefficient.
   - The threshold vanishes at |J̃| = ½: the window edge.
3. **Projected neutral algebra** (§5.3).
   - Two-point functions: ⟨Φ̃ⁱ†_R Φ̃ʲ_R⟩ = ⟨Φ̃ⁱ†_L Φ̃ʲ_R⟩ = δ_ij 2^{−4Δ_i}Γ(2Δ_i+1) (5.32; App. D, eqs. D.6–D.8). This equals the
     LMRS short-paper eq. (10), Γ(1+2Δ)/4^{2Δ}, as an independent check.
   - Three-point function: 2^{−2(Δ_i+Δ_j+Δ_k)} Γ(2Δ_i+1)Γ(2Δ_j+1)Γ(2Δ_k+1)/Γ(1+Δ_i+Δ_j+Δ_k) (App. D, after D.9).
   - **The product closes:** Φ̃ⁱ_R Φ̃ʲ_R = Σ_k A^{ij}_k Φ̃ᵏ_R (5.20), with
     A^{ij}_k = C_{ij k̄} 4^{Δ_k−Δ_i−Δ_j} Γ(1+2Δ_i)Γ(1+2Δ_j)/Γ(1+Δ_i+Δ_j+Δ_k) (5.21).
     C_{ij k̄} is the matter three-point coefficient (5.22). The sum includes the identity (Δ_k = 0).
   - Non-commutativity comes only from C_{ij k̄} ≠ C_{ji k̄}.
   - The matter vacuum is the unique tracial state, so Ã_{R,0} is **Type II₁** (§5.3, text around p. 62).
   - Ã_{L,0} is the commutant, identified with the right action of the algebra on itself (5.43).
4. **Charged operators and BPS counts** (§5.4).
   - The right-boundary ground-state space is ⊕_{|J_R|<½} H_matt^{(J_R)} (5.44). A charged primary of charge q_i maps J_R
     → J_R + 2q_i.
   - Two-point function (5.46), with a cos(π(J_R+2q_i)) factor and Γ(Δ+1±q)Γ(Δ+½±(J_R+q))/(πΓ(2Δ+1)).
   - The tracial state is |MAX⟩ = Σ_{J_R} √cos(πJ_R) |Ω^{(J_R)}⟩ (5.47–5.60).
   - Tr Π_{J_R} = cos(πJ_R)/Σ_{J′} cos(πJ′) (5.62). This is the known BPS density per R-charge, recovered from the operator
     algebra alone.
5. **Dimensions of representations** (5.64–5.65): d = cos(πJ_L)/Σ cos(πJ_R).
6. **App. E.** JT plus matter in a highest-weight gauge, which "connects nicely" to the double-scaled SYK chord Hilbert
   space (BBNR-type refs. [33, 34]).
   - The inner product has kernel (cosh((χ₋−χ̃₋)/2))^{−2λ} (E.7). It is positive because its Fourier transform is
     2^{2λ}Γ(λ±is)/Γ(2λ) (E.11).
   - Read only up to p. 84. The bosonic case only; no N=2 version is given there, as far as we read.

## Limitations
- The factor property is assumed (§5.5): a central operator acting only on the ground states is not excluded.
- Only probe matter on the disk; no wormhole or higher-genus contributions.
- The nonzero-energy algebra is argued by analogy with the bosonic case, not proven (§5.5).
- Pure super-JT, not SYK: no finite-N or q-dependence.

## Relationship to our project
- **Exact zero-energy count per multiplet.**
  - Gravity: exactly one simultaneous ground state per matter SU(1,1|1) multiplet with |2u − J_R| < ½, *including
    long multiplets*.
  - Microscopics (CV 2026, D6–D10): an uplift supplies one new BPS state per admissible source.
  - [interpretation] A natural candidate dictionary: an uplift source ↔ a matter multiplet; the window condition
    |2u − J_R| < ½ ↔ our admissible window. Unverified. It needs the matter charge of E (2/q̂, D11) and the sector
    charges of D10 put into PW's variables.
- **Window edge.** Result 2 is the canonical-quantization version of the LMRS (33) gap: the continuum threshold closes at
  |J| = ½. This supports the gravitational reading of the D10 resonant regime (see the short-LMRS note and synthesis
  §1, 2026-10-27).
- **The decoder as an element of a Type II₁ algebra.**
  - Our O_T = P_B Π_T P_B is a projected operator. If Π_T were a matter primary, PW's product rule (5.20–5.21) would give
    all its moments: iterate the product, then take the identity component.
  - Π_T = 1 − n_{N′} is a **composite**, not a primary. So PW do not directly predict the Jacobi law; they give the
    planar/crossing structure for primaries. Bridging the two means decomposing Π_T into projected primaries, which is
    open.
- **cos(πJ_R) weights.** These are the gravitational version of the per-charge BPS counts. In N=2 SYK, CV eq. (11) gives
  the q = 3 index per Z_q sector ∝ cos(π(N−2r)/6), which has the same cos(πj) form with j = (N−2r)/(2q̂).
  - [interpretation] PW's trace (5.62) is the algebraic version of the counting that the uplift bookkeeping (D10)
    reproduces sector by sector.
  - The exceptional states (D10) are index-invisible pairs. They are *not* captured by the cos(πJ) trace. This is
    consistent: the trace sees only the index-like density.
- **Chords.** App. E is the bridge between canonical JT plus matter and the chord Hilbert space. For a gravity-side
  derivation of the chord rules we use (BBNR, D7/D11), it is the closest rigorous source in our collection.
  - Only the bosonic version was read.
  - An N=2 highest-weight-gauge treatment would be the natural setting for roadmap item C1 (a chord derivation of the
    tower's protection). PW do not provide it.
