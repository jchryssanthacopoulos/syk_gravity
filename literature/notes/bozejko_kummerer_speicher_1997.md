# Bożejko, Kümmerer & Speicher (1997) — q-Gaussian processes: non-commutative and classical aspects

**Citation.** M. Bożejko, B. Kümmerer, R. Speicher, *q-Gaussian processes: non-commutative and classical aspects*,
Commun. Math. Phys. 185 (1997) 129–154, arXiv:funct-an/9604010 (30 Apr 1996). File:
`papers/bozejko_kummerer_speicher_1997.pdf` (28 pp). ("BKS".)

**Note written 2026-10-05.** Read: Introduction, §1 (q-Hermite polynomials), §2 (second quantization), §3
(q-Gaussian processes, Markov property). Skimmed: §4 (classical versions).

## Main question
Do the q-Gaussian processes of Frisch–Bourret (X_t = a_t + a_t*, with a_s a_t* − q a_t* a_s = c(s,t)) exist as
operator processes? Are they Markovian? Do they have classical versions?

## Physical system
Mathematical: the q-Fock space F_q(H), −1 < q < 1, the von Neumann algebra Γ_q(H) generated by
ω(f) = a(f) + a*(f), and the vacuum trace E = ⟨Ω, ·Ω⟩.

## Important definitions
- **q-Hermite polynomials** H_n^{(q)}: x H_n = H_{n+1} + [n]_q H_{n−1} (Def. 1.9).
- **Wick products.** H_n(ω(f))Ω = f^{⊗n} for ‖f‖ = 1 (Prop. 2.9).
- **Second quantization.** For a contraction T: H → H′, F_q(T) acts as T^{⊗n} on the n-particle space, and Γ_q(T)
  is defined by (Γ_q(T)X)Ω = F_q(T)(XΩ) (§2).
- **q-OU process:** the q-Gaussian process with covariance e^{−|t−s|} (Def. 3.5).

## Main assumptions
−1 < q < 1, real Hilbert spaces, and contractions as morphisms.

## Main analytical results
1. **Thm. 1.10.**
   - (1) The q-Hermite polynomials are orthogonal for ν_q on [−2/√(1−q), 2/√(1−q)], with x = 2cosθ/√(1−q).
   - (2) **Rogers' q-Mehler kernel:**
     p_r^{(q)}(x,y) = Σ_n r^n H_n(x)H_n(y)/[n]_q! = (r²;q)_∞ / |(re^{i(φ+ψ)};q)_∞ (re^{i(φ−ψ)};q)_∞|².
2. **Thm. 2.11.** Γ_q(T) exists for every contraction T. It is unital, completely positive and trace-preserving. For
   an isometry it is a homomorphism; for an orthogonal projection it is a conditional expectation.
3. **§3.**
   - Conditional expectations onto past algebras are second quantizations of Hilbert-space projections.
   - Hence Markovianity is a Hilbert-space property, independent of q (Props. 3.8–3.9).
   - q-Brownian motion, the q-Brownian bridge and the q-OU process are Markov (Cor. 3.10).
   - The q-OU process is the only stationary Markov q-Gaussian process (Prop. 3.11).
4. **§4.** Classical Markov versions exist that reproduce **time-ordered** correlations only (Def. 4.1).

## Important equations for us
Thm. 1.10(2), Prop. 2.9, Thm. 2.11, Def. 3.5, Def. 4.1.

## Numerical methods
None.

## Limitations
Mathematical framework only. No SYK, no supersymmetry, no ground-state projectors.

## Relationship to our project
1. **The rigorous form of the "x per chord" rule.**
   - Take X = ω(h) and Y = ω(h_x) with ⟨h, h_x⟩ = x. The conditional expectation of g(Y) onto vN(X) is Γ_q of the
     projection onto Rh, which acts as x^n on the n-particle component.
   - Therefore τ(f(X)g(Y)) = Σ_n x^n ⟨f_n, g_n⟩_q, where f_n is the n-chord component of f(X)Ω.
   - In DSSYK language (BINT/Lin 2022), F_q(Rh) is the chord Hilbert space and n is the chord number. So
     E tr f(H) g(H_x) = ⟨f| x^{N̂} |g⟩ in double scaling.
   - Our finite-N statement (classical Mehler over Gaussian couplings, chaos order = number of cross-copy Wick pairs)
     is the exact finite-N counterpart.
2. **The q-Mehler kernel is BINT's matter vertex (5.4) squared, with q̃ → r.** The OU correlation parameter x
   therefore plays exactly the role of a matter weight q̃ = q^Δ, giving the dictionary x ↔ q^{Δ_eff}.
3. **T₄^OU is an out-of-time-order correlator of the q-OU process.**
   - The four-arc function τ(f(X)g(Y)f(X)g(Y)) has operator times (s,t,s,t), which is not time-ordered. So it is not
     determined by the Markov transition kernel (Def. 4.1 covers time-ordered correlations only).
   - The time-ordered rearrangement τ(f²(X)g²(Y)) is a two-arc (Mehler) quantity.
   - The difference between the two is a genuinely non-commutative, crossed-channel quantity. This underlies
     treating the decoder remainder R as crossed/OTOC-like (see `docs/derivations.md` D5).
4. Applying this to N = 2 SUSY SYK needs the analogous second-quantization statement for BBNR's oriented-chord Fock
   space. It is expected by multilinearity but not proven in any paper we have.
